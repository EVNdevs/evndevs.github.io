# Changelog

## 0.2.50 (2026-09-27)
- **Firmware (needs the firmware of this release): a motor that cogs no longer flings its short moves after a calibration or a coast.** The open item of 0.2.49 is found and fixed. It was never the feed-forward's phase: from a settled detent rest (any coast or brake after motion; a same-session `calibrate()` ends in one) the Pololu 25D's rotor breaks away 10-16 ms late under the friction pre-load of a move from rest (0.8 x its 1.48 V breakaway) while its running friction is 0.10 V, and the excess push threw the shaft two or three detent pitches past a 2-10 deg target; after a reboot the rotor happened to rest off its detent and the same moves were gentle, which is why every bench had looked fine. On a rotor that cogs the pre-load is now the breakaway less the cogging feed-forward's amplitude (the feed-forward carries the detent as a function of position). On the bench (port 1, library Pololu, 8.08 V) the short set after a spin: 12-15 deg mean overshoot before, 1.2-1.4 after, every move done; after a reboot 3.1 -> 0.7 (short) and 1.5 -> 0.4 (fast). CHR-GM16 (0.14 / 0.21 deg) and EV3 Medium unchanged by construction (they do not cog). The 0.2.49 workaround (reboot after calibrating) is no longer needed.
- **Firmware: a `run_angle()` / `run_target()` from rest no longer starts with a step to the breakaway voltage.** The friction pre-load (0.8 x the calibrated breakaway) now includes the profile's acceleration feed-forward instead of adding it on top, and never drops below the calibrated kinetic friction. Short moves (2-10 deg) on a free EV3 Medium overshoot less (mean 1.41 -> 1.24 deg and 1.50 -> 1.30 deg on two units, five alternating runs each) and report `done()` sooner; EV3 Large 0.47 -> 0.32 deg; long moves, `run()` and `track_target()` unchanged.
- `evn._cogging(port, amp_mv)` (a bench hook) can override the cogging feed-forward's amplitude until the next calibration; `tools/bench/mpy_short_trace.py` traces the short set's moves at eight target phases. The Python heap is 144 KB (was 145).

## 0.2.49 (2026-09-27)
- **Firmware (needs the firmware of this release): `calibrate()` finds out whether a motor's rotor cogs, so a custom motor with magnetic detents is controlled like the Pololu 25D.** The calibration now ends with 20 short kicks, noting where the shaft comes to rest after each: a rotor with detents always rests in one, so the rests line up at the detent period (the Pololu 25D: every 8 encoder edges), while a LEGO motor, the JGA25 and the CHR-GM16 rest anywhere and are reported as not cogging. A **custom** motor that cogs then gets what the library Pololu has — the detents cancelled while it moves (the push is its breakaway minus its running friction) and the shaft resting in its nearest detent at the end of a move, with half a detent pitch of `done()` tolerance — without a library entry: the Pololu 25D configured as a custom motor measured 8 edges and ran the endpoint move sets like the library configuration. A library motor keeps its library figure; the measurement only checks it. `evn.calibration(port)` reports `cogging_edges` (what the port runs on), `cogging_measured`, `cogging_confidence` and `cogging_note`, and the Board view's motor tooltip says when a rotor cogs. `calibrate()` takes about 11 s instead of 7. The calibration record's format changes: after going back to an older firmware, configure and calibrate the motors again.
- **Firmware: `done()`'s default tolerance follows a calibration made in the background.** After `calibrate(wait=False)` finished, an open `Motor` kept the tolerance it was built with; it now follows what the port runs at once.
- **Firmware: `Motor(port, model=...)` on a port configured as a custom motor of the same class no longer keeps the custom motor's calibration numbers** (the record is not applied to the library motor, and now its numbers are not left running either).

- **Firmware: the cogging feed-forward's phase no longer drifts after a coast.** The feed-forward that cancels a rotor's detents takes its phase from where the shaft rests after coasting freely. The estimator parks a resting shaft's position at the far edge of the encoder step it stopped in, half a step past where the rotor can be known to be, and after one free coast (or a `calibrate()` in the same session) the feed-forward pushed with that error: a short move's overshoot on the Pololu 25D rose from about 3 deg to 14 deg until the next reboot, whose seed happens to sit mid-step. The phase is now the middle of the step, which is where the boot already put it. **Still open:** a `calibrate()` of a motor that cogs, followed by moves in the same session, still overshoots short moves by 10-14 deg on the Pololu 25D (13.6 deg before this change, 9.9 after; 3.1 after a reboot; every move finishes) - the phase value is not the cause (it reads the same after the calibration as after a reboot), something the live calibration leaves behind is, and the next release chases it. Until then: **reboot the board after calibrating a motor that cogs** (`evn.reset()`, or the reset button), as the calibration page keeps the result.
- The hold-trace bench prints only after its motor has coasted; the simulator's calibration takes the board's 11 s.

## 0.2.48 (2026-09-27)
- **Firmware (needs the firmware of this release): no false speed on the first encoder edge after a long rest.** The encoder's PIO program counts the clock cycles since the last edge in a word whose sign says which way that edge went; after 139.6 s with no edge the count wraps and the sign reads as the other direction. Since 0.2.42 (encoder phase tables per direction) the idle ticks then took the other direction's table for the step's bounds, and the first edge after a rest of more than 140 s was clamped against those stale bounds: one 1 ms sample of a false speed (405 deg/s in the host model of the rig's port 4, whose encoder has hysteresis). That sample reached `speed()` while the motor coasts, the legacy PID law's speed and the controller's starting speed if a move began on that very tick; the default controller's observer runs on the position, so a move that starts later was never affected. A tick with no edge now keeps the last edge's direction, so the bounds and the next edge always come from one table. The age of the last edge (what tells a drive base its wheel is engaged) is clamped at 8.7 s instead of overflowing. Modelled in the host test `tools/host_tests/test_quadrature.py` (0 deg/s where the old rule read 405); on the board a move and a coasting kick after a 150 s rest behave as after 5 s, with no missed tick (the one-tick sample itself is below what a program can resolve). Found by the host model of the estimator written this release.
- **Docs: seventeen contradictions fixed** across the API reference, the stubs and hovers, and the getting-started guide: every library motor's default speed and acceleration limits and `full_speed()` figures (a table for all six), the CHR-GM16's 9 V cap ("never binds" on a 2S pack), `run(100)` after `calibrate()` (not clamped: the limit becomes the measured full speed), `target_tolerances()` defaults per motor, `Stop.COAST_SMART`'s 2 deg window, the Geekservo 360 in the Servo row, and "a smaller API" dropped from the extended peripherals (they are supported like the standard ones; EVN just does not stock them).
- **Bench: `tools/bench/mpy_endpoint_ab.py` can average out "phase luck".** With `PHASES` set, each port coasts to a zero and runs the set five times from aims spread over the encoder cycle, so the pooled mean no longer depends on where the shaft happened to stand; the default stays one pass, the figure every release's A/B numbers were taken with.

## 0.2.47 (2026-09-27)
- **Firmware (needs the firmware of this release): a motor that cogs rests in its nearest detent.** The Pololu 25D's rotor has a magnetic detent every 6.2 deg at the output, and a target that falls between two detents cannot be held there: the controller's push per degree is a thirtieth to a fiftieth of what a detent pulls with, so the end-of-move push built up until the shaft broke away, snapped a whole detent past the target, and the push built up the other way - the 4-8 deg wobble after `done()` that the bench showed on every short move. Now the controller lets the shaft settle into the nearest detent: on a library motor that cogs, the endpoint band and the default `done()` tolerance are half the detent pitch plus a quarter encoder step (3.3 deg on the Pololu 25D; every other motor keeps 1 deg / two encoder steps). On the bench (both Pololu units, fast + short + long sets, 96 moves per image, 8.2 V): the wobble after `done()` in the bench's 300 ms window fell from 5.7-7.8 deg peak-to-peak to 4.6-5.7 (the window still holds the settle; the trace shows the hold itself quiet at 0 V within 200 ms, where the reference build kept 0.5-1.25 V standing on the stalled motor), every move done, the mean error 1.0-1.5 deg either way (the shaft rests in a detent: up to half a pitch from the target) and the arrival's overshoot unchanged (the shaft crosses its last crest at low speed either way). The cogging feed-forward keeps cancelling the detents while the shaft moves, so speed control is unchanged. For positioning finer than half a detent this motor needs a higher gear ratio; the docs say so.

## 0.2.45 (2026-09-26)
- **Firmware (needs the firmware of this release): EVN Extended Peripherals.** A second tier beside the standard peripherals: devices the firmware drives natively with a smaller API, identified by an ID string, an ID register or a handshake (never by the address alone), read from a cache refreshed in the background and found again after an unplug. Five to start: **`HiTechnicColorSensor(port)`**, the HiTechnic NXT Color Sensor V2 or V1 (`version()` says which) through an NXT cable adapter: `color()`, `color_match()`, `detectable_colors()` as `ColorSensor`, the sensor's own `color_number()` (0..17), `rgb()`, `hsv()`, `reflection()`, and on a V2 `ambient()` (LED off), `raw()` and `mains(50 / 60)`; **`HiTechnicCompass(port)`**: `heading()` in whole degrees, `north()`, and the sensor's own calibration `calibrate()` / `calibrate_stop()` / `calibrating()`; **`HuskyLens(port)`**, the DFRobot AI camera in I2C mode: `algorithm(HuskyLens.OBJECT_TRACKING)` and the six other algorithms, `blocks(id)` as `(x, y, width, height, id)`, `arrows(id)`, `count()`, `learned()`, `frame()`, `learn(id)`, `forget()`; **`VL53L1X(port)`**, ST's time-of-flight distance sensor that reaches 4 m (identified by its model ID after the VL53L0X and TCS34725 that share its address are ruled out): `distance()` in mm (`None` when not valid), `status()`, `raw()`, `distance_mode('long' / 'short')`, `timing_budget(15..500)`, long mode at 33 ms by default; **`TCS3430(port)`**, the ams-OSRAM XYZ colour and light sensor with an infrared channel (told from the APDS-9960 at the same address by its ID): `color()`, `color_match()`, `detectable_colors()` and `hsv()` with the same matcher as the other colour sensors, `calibrate_black()` / `calibrate_white()` and `black_reference()` / `white_reference()` (below), `xyz()`, `raw()`, `ir()`, `xy()` (uncalibrated chromaticity), `saturated()`, `gain(1..128)`, `integration_time(2.78..711.7)`, one 2.78 ms cycle at 64x by default. Bench-validated on the rig (a HiTechnic Color V2, a HiTechnic Compass and a HuskyLens: 54 / 54 and 42 / 42 checks, 0 bus errors, the other devices on the bus unaffected; a VL53L1X on port 1 and a TCS3430 on port 16); the V1 colour sensor is supported from its register map. Documented in the API reference (*EVN Extended Peripherals*), `help()`, the stubs and hovers, five snippets (`evn-hitechnic-color`, `evn-hitechnic-compass`, `evn-huskylens`, `evn-vl53l1x`, `evn-tcs3430`) and a new *Extended Peripherals* group of examples, `examples/extended_peripherals/`, with a folder per device (`01_hitechnic_color/` to `05_tcs3430/`), each with a minimal and a complete program in Python and blocks (the Examples tree and the site's example listing show a group as a heading over its folders). They are not `DataLog` sources.
- **Firmware: the TCS3430 sees colours after a black and a white.** Its reading stays in the sensor's linear XYZ: `calibrate_black()` (nothing in front, or a black target) is subtracted per channel, `calibrate_white()` (a white sheet under the module's own LED) divides each channel by the white's and scales it to D65, so a white target reads `s` 0, `v` 100; then the sRGB standard's inverse matrix gives the RGB behind `hsv()`, and `color()` / `color_match()` use the same matcher as `ColorSensor` (a CIELAB match was tried first and dropped: against Pybricks' full-brightness colours the lightness swamped the hue). `black_reference()` / `white_reference()` return the references as `(X, Y, Z)` per 1x-gain cycle, or `None`, and clear them with `None`; they live in the object's RAM (nothing stored on the board) and stay valid when `gain()` or `integration_time()` change. **Call `tcs.calibrate_black()` then `tcs.calibrate_white()` at the start of a program**: uncalibrated, the warm LED makes a white sheet read yellow and nothing in front read blue. Checked by hand on the rig (2.78 ms at 64x, targets about 1 cm): nothing NONE 1.00, white WHITE 1.00, red RED 0.98, green GREEN 0.75, blue BLUE 0.91–0.93. The example, the `evn-tcs3430` snippet and a *calibrate TCS3430 black / white* block show the two lines.
- **Firmware: the HiTechnic colour sensor takes the same black and white.** `HiTechnicColorSensor` has `calibrate_black()`, `calibrate_white()`, `black_reference()` and `white_reference()` too, V1 and V2: a two-point calibration of its active-mode red, green and blue (0..255, ambient cancelled), each channel (reading − black) / (white − black), clamped 0..100 %, before `hsv()`, `color()` and `color_match()` (`rgb()` stays the sensor's own). The references are `(R, G, B)` in counts, kept in the object's RAM; `calibrate_white()` raises `ValueError` when a channel is not at least 10 % above the black, and a V2 is switched to active mode first. Shown in the complete example, the `evn-hitechnic-color` snippet and a *calibrate HiTechnic colour sensor black / white* block.
- **Firmware: a clock per I2C port.** `evn.I2C(port, freq=100000)` slows one port (10000..400000 Hz) for a device that needs it, and `I2C(port).freq()` reads the rate a port runs at; every other port stays at 400 kHz, and the end of a program puts them all back. The HiTechnic sensors run their port at 100 kHz by themselves (at 400 kHz they failed about one read in 800).
- **Firmware: `evn.I2C` reaches addresses 0x01..0x07.** `scan()` lists 0x01..0x77 and every call accepts them (0x00, the general call, stays refused): 0x01 is the NXT's 8-bit address 0x02, where every HiTechnic and LEGO NXT I2C sensor sits.
- **Blocks: an *Extended* category** with a sub-category per device: *set up* blocks, the HiTechnic colour sensor's *calibrate black / white*, colour, *sees*, colour number, reflection and channels, the HiTechnic compass's heading and *set heading*, the HuskyLens's algorithm, objects seen, first block (x, y, width, height or ID; -1 when it sees none) and *learn*, the VL53L1X's distance (-1 when not valid) and *long / short range*, and the TCS3430's *calibrate black / white*, colour, *sees*, X / Y / Z / infrared / hue / saturation / brightness and gain.
- **The *Board* view names the new devices** by their ID (HiTechnic Color V1 / V2 and Compass by their ID strings, read at 100 kHz; the HuskyLens by its handshake; the VL53L1X by its model ID, read only after the VL53L0X and TCS34725 ID registers at the same address; the TCS3430 by its ID register), reads them live with the eye button and offers their settings on the pencil button (the V2's mains frequency, the compass's heading, the camera's algorithm and *learn*, the VL53L1X's distance mode and timing budget, the TCS3430's gain and integration time). The data logger does not list them: `evn.DataLog` records motors and standard peripherals.
- **The API reference is split into pages.** It had grown to one page of 1,700 lines; `docs/API.md` is now a short index (the ports, the units, the differences from Pybricks, safety, and a table of every section) and the reference is five pages: *The board, the motors, the drive base and the pose*, *Standard peripherals: sensors*, *Standard peripherals: displays, lights, servo and Bluetooth*, *Extended peripherals* and *Ports, programs, the data logger and timing*. Every section moved whole. On the site each part is its own page with previous / next links and a way back to the index, and a link of the one-page era (`#/api/<section>`, from an older extension build or a bookmark) is forwarded to the part that now holds that section. **EVN: Open online documentation for the symbol under the cursor** opens the right part.

## 0.2.42 (2026-09-26)
- **Firmware (needs the firmware of this release): the encoder is calibrated in each direction of turning, on every motor.** A quadrature encoder's four edges never sit exactly a quarter cycle apart, and `calibrate()` has measured where they are since 0.2.20 - but only with the 1 kHz control tick, which cannot time an edge that shares a millisecond with another. That left the Pololu 25D (eight edges per millisecond at calibration speed) and any encoder with a narrow phase running on the assumed even spacing, and an encoder whose edges sit at different angles depending on the direction of turning (Hall-sensor hysteresis: one of the bench EV3 Mediums reads 8/106/9/133 substeps of 256 one way and 105/9/133/9 the other, 0.43 deg apart) was rejected as unusable. Now `calibrate()` times every edge with a microsecond poll at the tail of each of its four pulses (both directions, two speeds; the calibration takes about 7 s now and the shaft moves up to about a turn and a half each way) and keeps **one table per direction**; the controller places a stopped shaft by the table of the direction it arrived in, and a reversal is read at zero speed. Timing alone cannot say how far one direction's cycle sits from the other's, so the two tables are pinned at the same edge and, when that pinned channel is the one whose edges move (they never fire early), the reverse table is re-gauged onto the other channel; two tables that agree within 4 substeps of 256 are averaged into one, so a motor whose edges read the same both ways runs exactly as before. On that Medium the end-of-move error over the same 16-move set fell from a mean of 0.38 deg on 0.2.41 (two cycles; every forward move parked 0.49 deg past its target) to 0.12 deg (three cycles, one of them every move within 0.11 deg); the other bench motors, whose edges read the same both ways, are unchanged within the run-to-run scatter, and every LEGO move finished in every cycle. `evn._encoder_table(port)` now returns both tables `(table_fwd, table_rev, widths_fwd, widths_rev, note, pin_state, step)`; `evn._encoder_phases(port, duty, install=True)` installs the direction it spun. The calibration page is format 7 (older pages load with their one table both ways); **firmware older than this refuses a page written by it whole** - the ports then boot on the compiled fallback (EV3 Large on 1-2, EV3 Medium on 3-4) with no calibration - so after a downgrade configure each port's motor again (the *Board* view or `evn.configure_motor()`) and run `calibrate()` on it.

## 0.2.41 (2026-09-26)
- **Firmware (needs the firmware of this release): the Pololu 25D 9.7:1 HP 12V joins the motor library.** Pololu item #4842, the 9.7:1 Metal Gearmotor 25Dx63L mm HP 12V with the 48 CPR encoder: `evn.configure_motor(port, "pololu25d_9_7")` (or `"Pololu 25D 9.7:1 HP 12V"`, also as `Motor(port, model=...)` and in the *Board* view's motor picker). 464.64 encoder counts per output revolution (48 per motor turn x the exact 9.68:1), rated 12 V (above the battery, so the port is never capped), no-load 1000 rpm = 6000 deg/s at 12 V and about 3500 deg/s on the battery, EV3 Medium control class, default limits 3000 deg/s and 20000 deg/s² (the speed follows the ramp exactly up to 20000 on the bench units). Its encoder counts against its drive on the EVN cable, so `calibrate()` it once before the first move: the calibration finds that and flips the port. Its stall current on a full battery (about 3.4 A) is above the port's 3 A continuous rating: do not hold it stalled.
- **Firmware: positioning on a motor that cogs.** The Pololu's rotor has six magnetic detents per motor turn (one every 6.2 deg at the output): coasted, it only ever comes to rest in one, and leaving one costs about 1.2 V. On 0.2.40 `run_angle()` on this motor parked 6-13 deg short and never returned (0 of 8 moves finished on one unit). Three changes, each measured on both bench units: **a cogging feed-forward** cancels the detents' pull (its size is what `calibrate()` measures as breakaway minus running friction, its position is taken from the shaft resting coasted for 0.3 s: at boot, and then after every coast in which the shaft moved freely - a shaft held still while coasted is not trusted; it works below about 900 deg/s, where it matters, and fades out above); **the end-of-move push** notices a shaft that springs back after a step and pushes on (only on a motor whose control could not otherwise reach its own breakaway voltage: no LEGO motor or JGA25 qualifies); **`done()`'s default tolerance** is two steps of the motor's own encoder and at least 1 deg (1.55 deg here; it was 1 deg, less than the controller's own endpoint band on this encoder, so a move could stop 1.15 deg off and never report done). Result: 192 of 192 moves of 2-1080 deg at 50-3000 deg/s finish, mean error 0.7-1.0 deg, worst 4.5 deg. EV3 Large and EV3 Medium (free shafts, and Mediums driving wheels): unchanged, 384 moves against 0.2.40 (the feed-forward never runs on them; the new push rule stays off below its threshold). A `target_tolerances()` position below two encoder steps can again leave a move never reporting done.
- **Firmware: `angle()` exact on an encoder whose counts per turn are not a whole number.** The conversion rounded the Pololu's 29736.96 substeps per turn to 29737: 0.5 deg of drift per 1000 turns. LEGO and JGA25 geometries are bit-for-bit unchanged.
- **Simulator:** the JGA25-370 now reports its real control class, EV3 Large (it said EV3 Medium).

## 0.2.40 (2026-09-26)
- **The board's data logs and storage in the *Board* view.** *Files on the board* now starts with a **Storage** row (`Storage · 10.6 MB free of 11.0 MB (96 %)`; the tooltip gives the bytes in use and how much of that is data logs). When less than a tenth of the file system or less than 256 kB is free, the row and *Files on the board* show a warning with "- low", and one message offers **Download all data logs**: a recording `evn.DataLog` cannot save when the program ends is lost. Under it the **data** folder (the board's `/data`, where `DataLog.save()` and the autosave write) lists the logs newest first with their sizes (`data · 3 data logs, 412 kB`). Click a log, or its cloud button, to download it into the **Data** folder of your projects folder, where a `.csv` opens in the data viewer at once (*Replace* / *Keep both* when the name is taken, the copy becoming `name (2).csv`); every file in the board root has the same button. The **data** row downloads all logs with one button and deletes them all from its right-click menu (confirmed once); each log has its own delete button. It works over the live console, with a progress notification of the bytes, or over mpremote when the console is off and the port is free, and the list is re-read after every download or delete.
- **Firmware (needs the firmware of this release): a run of identical samples is two rows.** A sample bit-identical to its reading's previous row no longer adds a row: the run's first row stays and its last row's time stamp moves forward to the newest sample (the owner's rule: "any n data points from 1 source get compressed into 2 if data unchanged"). The file and the chart show the value flat over that window, and every sample in it was taken and compared: it is not a dead-band, and a change of one bit is recorded. A streamed reading that does not change costs about one row per heartbeat. `log()` rows are never folded. `DataLog.info()` counts the folded samples per channel in `same`; the file's `# channel:` line ends `| 1081 rows of 1221 samples`; the extension's own recordings end with a `# samples taken: ...` line and the message says "1,290 rows of 2,165 samples over 2.1 s". The data viewer's mean, standard deviation and slope now weight each row by the time it holds, so a flat window counts for its whole span, and its rate is the typical gap between rows; halving is unchanged, and a reading's memory fills more slowly.
- **Console protocol:** `info` carries `data` (the files in `/data` with their sizes), a new `get` command sends a root file or `data/<name>` as base64 `file` lines and a `done` with its size, `rm` accepts `data/<name>`, and the `log` stop's `done` carries `taken` (the samples each channel took); the browser IDE's simulated board does the same and ships a sample log in its `data` folder.

## 0.2.38 (2026-09-25)
- **Firmware: a `DriveBase` builds and owns its `Pose`.** `DriveBase(left, right, wheel_diameter=62.4, axle_track=170, imu=3)` (and `compass=`, `declination=`) builds the pose estimator from the base's own geometry: the two motor ports, the wheel diameter and axle track, each motor's `positive_direction` and the motors' gears. - **Firmware: the 1 kHz tick never branches into flash, and a gate proves it after every link** (B-004). Two callees of the tick were flash-resident and invisible from the source: `time_us_64()` behind an inline wrapper, fetched on every tick before the exec stopwatch (so `exec_max` never showed it; the tick period read 991/1009 us instead of 1000/1000), and the `memcpy` the compiler emits for two struct copies, whose SDK shim sat in flash. ## 0.2.15 (2026-09-18)

**Every one of the fifteen EVN Standard Peripherals is now bench-validated** (the per-device dates are in `docs/STANDARD_PERIPHERALS.md` §1). The documentation, the stubs, the snippets, the examples and the board's own `help()` no longer say "not validated on hardware" anywhere, and the stub's signatures were re-derived from the firmware bindings method by method — about a hundred of them disagreed with the C (a keyword the binding does not take, a getter typed as a setter, an `int` where the firmware returns a `float`, an optional argument shown as required).

**The I2C transport recovers by itself.** A transaction that timed out used to leave the RP2040's controller mid-transfer with bytes in its FIFOs, so the next read on that bus returned a stale byte and reported success (on the rig, a colour sensor's ID read 0x00 instead of 0x4D after a forced overrun). The layer now aborts, resets the controller, clocks a held SDA free with nine SCL pulses and a STOP, and re-initialises the bus after every timeout; it drops the multiplexer's channel cache after any failed transaction, so a multiplexer that lost its channel is re-selected instead of never; and it sizes the deadline to the transfer (1 ms + 40 µs per byte, at least 5 ms), so a long transfer no longer times out for being long. The three outcomes are now distinguishable from Python: `OSError(ENODEV)` — nothing answered at that address; `OSError(EIO)` — the device answered but refused a byte; `OSError(ETIMEDOUT)` — the bus was held and has been reset. `evn.I2C(port).stats()` reports `((errors, recoveries, stuck), (...))` per bus.

- **`evn.I2C`**: `scan()` never lists 0x70; 0x70 (the board's two multiplexers, which answer on every port) is refused on every port and 0x6A on port 16 (the battery charger) with `ValueError` — a stray write to either would have re-routed the whole bus or reprogrammed charging. `writeto()` takes 1..4096 bytes. New `stats()`. `_deadline(us)` is a private bench hook, documented as such.
- **`Compass`**: new `calibrate_cancel()` — a refused `calibrate_stop()` raises `ValueError` and **keeps collecting**, so a program can turn the sensor further and stop again, or give up cleanly. New `overrun()` (the QMC5883L's dropped-measurement flag) and `self_test(negative=False, raw=False)` (HMC5883L, at the gain the datasheet prescribes). `data_rate()` and `range()` return floats on both chips. `bias()` is refused below 4.0 Ga, where the strap saturates. The closed-object message is now `"Compass is closed"`, and a second `Compass(port)` on an open port keeps the calibration and the `north()` reference.
- **`Bluetooth` and `UART`**: new `readline(timeout=5000)` on both — the next line without its trailing `b"\n"` and without a `b"\r"` in front of it, or `None` on timeout; only the line leaves the receive buffer, so bytes that arrived behind it stay for the next `read()`. `timeout=0` never waits, a negative timeout raises `ValueError`, and a line longer than 255 bytes never completes: `readline()` times out and `overflow()` counts what was dropped. New `overflow()` on both (read-and-clear), and `UART.repl()` was missing from the stub.
- **`ColorSensor`**: `gain()`, `integration_time()`, `wait_time()` and `thresholds()` now return only once a sample measured under the new setting exists. `interrupt()` documented as `True` at the default persistence of 0 ("every cycle") — pass 1 or more for a flag that means "the clear count left the window".
- **`GestureSensor`**: `read_gesture(timeout=5000)` accepts the keyword the docs always promised (it raised `TypeError` before); a negative timeout raises `ValueError`. `status()[4]` is the chip's `GCONF4.GMODE` bit. `gesture_detail()`'s first three values are direction names or `None`, not integers.
- **`ADC`**: the default range is **±4.096 V** and the documentation no longer says ±6.144 V. `raw()`, `voltage()`, `read()` and `age()` take the input positionally.
- **`EnvSensor`**: documented that normal mode delivers a reading every measurement time plus the standby (40.5 ms at the defaults, ≈ 24.7 per second), while `measurement_time()` is the datasheet's worst case.
- **`TouchArray`**: the three `thresholds()` forms are documented as the binding implements them — `thresholds()` reads channel 0, `thresholds(3)` reads channel 3, `thresholds(12, 6)` sets every channel **including the proximity channel 12**, and `thresholds(3, channel=2)` raises `TypeError`. `overcurrent()` reports a fault seen since the object was opened, because the driver recovers from it by itself.
- **`Display`**: `text(col, row, s, invert=False)` accepts the keyword and returns the number of characters written; `on(enable=True)`, `zoom(enable=True)` and `all_on(enable=True)` take the argument optionally; `flip()` and `invert()` are getters as well as setters. Documented: the panel's RAM is not written while a hardware scroll runs (the first drawing call stops it and repaints, a new `scroll()` sends the stop first), `flip()` does not repaint, the constructor can take up to ≈ 740 ms on a freshly plugged panel, and `mirror()` is dropped by Ctrl-D.
- **`MatrixLED` / `SevenSegmentLED`**: `addr=None` picks the class default (0x71 / 0x74) and `addr=0x70` is refused with `"addr must be 0x71..0x77 (0x70 is the board's I2C multiplexer)"`. `SevenSegmentLED.number()` refuses an int outside −999..9999 and NaN / infinity instead of showing a saturated value; `MatrixLED.text(on=0)` is refused. The closed-object message names the class (`"MatrixLED is closed"`, `"SevenSegmentLED is closed"`) and a full pool says `"port %d: no free LED display slot (at most 4)"`.
- **`IMU`**: `tilt()` is documented with its real range, (−180, 180] on both axes, so an upside-down module is not read as level; `screen_orientation()` reports each event once; `dmp(rate=None)` keeps the rate in force; `stationary()` and `ready()` raise while the module is out; `settings(heading_correction=)` is clamped to 1..3600. The gyro filter values 250 Hz and 3600 Hz are refused (they select the chip's 8 kHz internal rate and break the DMP's time base), and an `evn.I2C` scan of the IMU's port pops one FIFO byte, which the driver heals.
- **`Servo.close()` and `UART.close()` are new.** A `Servo` object used to hold its channel until the next soft reset, so a program could not hand a servo port to an `RGBLED` strip (or re-open it with another profile) without Ctrl-D; a `UART` object held its serial port the same way. `close()` releases the claim on both — the servo's pulse stops and its pin goes low, the serial port keeps running and keeps its queued bytes — and every later call on the closed object raises `ValueError("Servo is closed")` / `ValueError("UART is closed")`. A second `UART` on a port an open object holds now says so (`OSError("serial port %d is already open")`), and a `Servo` constructor waits up to 10 ms for a just-closed strip to hand the channel back. `evn.I2C.scan()` also stops on a port whose probe timed out, instead of carrying the rest of the scan out against the bare bus.
- **`Servo` / `RGBLED`**: the servo channel has one owner — `RGBLED(n)` raises while a `Servo` object holds the port and the other way round; a servo port pulses nothing until the first `Servo` object is built; `disable()` is sticky but a new object drives its port again. `RGBLED`'s `invert` is keyword-only, and a blink or animate step below 2 ms is refused (the pattern is stepped by the 1 kHz poll).
- **Documentation and tooling**: `docs/API.md` and the README no longer describe the peripherals as pending a bench check; every peripheral snippet imports what it uses on its first line, and there are new snippets for the ADC, environment sensor, touch array, gesture sensor, LED matrix, seven-segment display, Bluetooth module and UART. `examples/08_standard_peripherals.py` was read against the bindings and every call that would have raised is fixed (`read_gesture(timeout=)`, `text(invert=)`, a seven-segment `text()` of letters the chip has no glyph for, an unguarded `calibrate_stop()`). The board's `help()` lists every class with its methods. The four block examples — `colour_line`, `spirit_level`, `compass_lights` and `bluetooth_grabber` — are named in the README and the getting-started guide, which also say that every peripheral has blocks.

## 0.1.3 (2026-09-16)

- Firmware rebuilt with the ADRC control law as the default (active disturbance rejection; self-calibrating, no per-motor tuning), `control.law()` to read or switch it (`"adrc"` / `"pid"`), and the observer at half bandwidth while holding at the endpoint (no more endpoint hunting on the EV3 Medium).
- Default limits at the fastest validated moves: acceleration 2400 deg/s² (EV3 Large) / 3000 deg/s² (EV3 Medium); a calibrated port's speed limit is its measured no-load speed when that is higher, so `run(100)` in percent mode is not clamped. The firmware caps every move at what the motor can do at the present battery voltage.
- `calibrate(wait=False)` starts a port without blocking; a later `calibrate()` on that port joins it, so several ports calibrate from one call.
- `done()` is true within 1° (was about 11°); `stalled()` / `load()` come from the ADRC's disturbance estimate (a calibrated unknown motor gets a working stall flag).
- `calibrate()` measures the motor's no-load speed as well (two-amplitude pulse test, about 4.5 s) and returns `(b0, tau_ms, v_break_mv, v_f_mv)`; a calibrated port's `full_speed()` is that measurement. `calibrate_speed()` removed (it duplicated `calibrate()`).
